Why This Narrative

Read this once, before Discovery.

This is an economics class. Why does the biggest writing assignment ask you to tell a story?

Writing forces you to work through your own reasoning. That's true in any class, and it's why Mind & Voice exists here. But there's a more specific reason too. Economics studies how people choose, and only a story can show a choice actually happening, with the alternative still in view. A definition states that people respond to trade-offs, but watching someone actually choose is what shows you what that means.

Why a decision, and not just a moment

Economics is the study of how people choose when they don't have enough of something — time, money, whatever the scarce thing is. A story about weather or luck doesn't have reasoning inside it, because nobody decided anything. A story about a real decision does, because a real decision means someone weighed an alternative and something tipped the balance.

This is why Discovery asks for a road not taken before it asks for anything else. Without a real alternative, what you have is just an event.

The story carries the reasoning — it doesn't illustrate it

A weak version of this assignment states the economic concept and then finds a story to match it. The concept comes first, and the story is decoration. The stronger version runs the other way: you tell the story straight, and a reader who knows the reasoning can see it sitting inside the facts, in a "why" that stays implicit. That's the version this assignment asks for. If you can name the concept in your first draft, take the label back out and see whether the scene still carries the weight without it.

You were there — the viva just follows

You watched this decision happen, or you made it yourself. Either way, you have something I can't get anywhere else in this conversation — the actual reasoning behind it, as you lived it. Discovery, The Scene, and Revision exist to help you get that reasoning onto the page clearly enough that the viva is just a continuation of the writing. Walk me through what was driving the choice, and I'll follow wherever it goes.
