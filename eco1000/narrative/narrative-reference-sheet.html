Narrative Reference Sheet

Keep this for the whole narrative unit: you'll use it again during Discovery, and when you write and revise The Scene, the personal narrative due later this semester.

The six elements

Every narrative answers these six questions, whether condensed to just the six or expanded to develop one or more of them further.

ElementWhat it covers
Agentthe person who acts — who they are, and anything about them that matters to the story
Actionthe sequence of events, in the order they happened
Timewhen it happened, and how long it took
Placewhere it happened
Mannerhow the action happened: the style or way it was carried out (calmly, secretly, in a rage)
Causewhy the agent acted: the motive or reason behind the action

Every one of these six is a place someone could challenge your story. If a reader doesn't believe the cause, they don't believe the story, no matter how well the action is told.

Manner vs. Cause, worth separating on purpose: Manner is how someone acted — the observable style of the action. Cause is why they acted — the motive behind it, which usually has to be inferred. A character apologizing quietly is Manner. Apologizing because they felt guilty is Cause. Quick test: "how" in front of the detail signals Manner, and "why" in front of it signals Cause.

The three attributes

What makes a narrative good, whichever mode you're writing in.

  • Brevity — start where the story actually needs to start. Leave out what the reader can infer.
  • Clarity — tell events in the order they happened, so the cause stays visible throughout.
  • Credibility — give the agent a motive a reader would actually believe.

Self-check questions

Ask these of any draft, yours or someone else's:

  1. Brevity — where does the story actually start? Is there an earlier moment that could have been left out because the reader would infer it anyway?
  2. Clarity — is the cause of the character's action visible, or does the reader have to guess?
  3. Credibility — is the motive believable? The events can be perfectly ordinary — what has to hold up is the reason behind them.
